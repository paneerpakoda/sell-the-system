# Product Design Incremental One Pager

The current design is [[Game Design Document]]. This page is the original one-pager.

## Problem Statement

How might we make a browser incremental about one product, where you design by hand, work a pile of broken flows, and go deeper into that same product, so a designer recognizes the job and an incremental player just wants the next upgrade?

## Recommended Direction

You have one product. The client is paying for the design. You start with no design tool. On each screen you set the colour, the corner radius, and the contrast yourself. Shipping that screen pays ordinary money. A System is not what you sell. A System takes one of those adjustments and does it for you, first as a head start, later on its own. Figma comes later, as the first real tool. Paper stays an undeveloped sketch.

Depth is how the game stays long. Finishing a layer opens a deeper cut of the same product: wireframe, then UI, then states, then edge cases, then the organization around it. Tools you already bought still matter. Something is buried under the last layer. That secret is not part of the first build.

This is a game people play for the climb. The games it sits next to are Find The Needle and A Game About Digging A Hole. Someone keeps playing because the pile and the upgrades feel good, and because the job being automated is recognizable if they happen to know it.

The hand action is opening one element and using the tool that appears: colour, corner radius, or contrast for that element. Systems take those tools over. After all three are on, an intern can be hired to use them, with a salary, a lower fee, and misses you have to fix. Numbers are in [[Game Parameters]] and on the dev panel. The loop follows [[Incremental Loop]]. Pay for a shipped design stays flat.

## Key Assumptions to Validate

- [ ] A person who does not design will push the screen for ten minutes because the pile and the upgrades feel good. Test with a gray-box browser page and one gesture.
- [ ] A designer will recognize the late joke: you stop designing and start reviewing someone worse. Test by describing only that beat, with no pitch around it.
- [ ] A small set of ticket patterns can fill the pile. Test with about eight templates before writing any bespoke tickets.
- [ ] One product is enough. A client roster would turn this back into the agency sims the research already set aside.

## MVP Scope

A browser page. One product, one screen at a time. You open each element and set colour, corner radius, and contrast from the tool that appears, then ship the design. Systems for those three come after a few shipments. The intern comes after the systems. Figma, Paper, and any leadership offer come after this.

No second layer, no secret, no design system, no client list, no pricing model.

## Not Doing (and Why)

- Advertising, marketing, pitches, and a client market. Already occupied, and set aside.
- A five-minute score attack. That is Design Studio Tycoon, and the length is the thing being refused.
- A real design tool. Spare time cannot build one, and an incremental does not need it.
- Feeding the product, changing the organizational weather, a currency of settled decisions, or a game where you only critique. Those verbs were left out.
- The buried secret and the organization layer. They are the long-climb promise. They are not how you find out whether the gesture is fun.
